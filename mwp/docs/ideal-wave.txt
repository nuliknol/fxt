› The fit_error is currently a metric on deviation from 'range' wave and 'trend' wave. Correct?
  If so, how is the ideal 'trend' and ideal 'trend' are defined? (specifically)


• Yes.

  For relative_waves, relative_waves_2_stage, and relative_waves_seeded, fit_error is the error of the
  winning class between:

  - trend
  - range

  So for each token:

  - compute trend_error
  - compute range_error
  - choose the smaller one
  - fit_error = min(trend_error, range_error)

  How the ideal trend is defined
  It is not one exact prototype candle sequence. It is defined by low penalty on these properties:

  - high relative_directional_efficiency_step
  - high relative_directional_efficiency_body
  - low relative_retracement_ratio
  - high relative_avg_body_efficiency
  - low relative_overlap_ratio
  - low sign_change_density
  - favorable relative_wick_bias_aligned
  - low continuous relative_trend_quality_raw

  In trainer/src/wave_dp.c, relative_trend_error is built from:

  - relative_strong_trend_error
  - relative_weak_trend_error

  and then:

  relative_trend_error = min(strong, weak) + move_penalty

  So the ideal trend is basically:

  - strong directional progress
  - low internal back-and-forth
  - low pullback
  - low body overlap
  - limited sign flips
  - reasonable wick behavior
  - enough absolute move

  In plain language:

  - clean move
  - mostly one direction
  - shallow retracement
  - little congestion

  How the ideal range is defined
  range_error is low when the token looks more sideways/choppy:

  - low relative_directional_efficiency_step
  - lower directional body efficiency
  - enough overlap
  - enough sign changes
  - enough retracement
  - not too much net directional progress

  In trainer/src/wave_dp.c, relative_range_error is low when:

  - DER_step is below relative_range_der_step_max
  - avg_body_efficiency is below relative_range_body_eff_max
  - overlap is at least relative_range_overlap_min
  - sign_change_density is at least relative_range_sign_flip_min
  - retracement is not too low
  - net move is not too large after normalization

  So the ideal range is:

  - overlapping candles
  - internal back-and-forth
  - less efficient directional move
  - more chop than displacement

  Short version

  Ideal trend:

  - high displacement efficiency
  - low retracement
  - low overlap
  - low chop
  - enough move

  Ideal range:

  - low displacement efficiency
  - higher overlap
  - more sign changes
  - more internal chop
  - less clean net progress

  So yes, fit_error is the deviation from those two class ideals, and whichever ideal the token is
  closer to becomes its class.

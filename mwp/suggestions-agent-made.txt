
• Yes. Given your objective, I’d focus less on “entry prediction” and more on state-aware exit control.

  What you already have is useful:

  - wave segmentation gives you a compressed market history
  - seeded trends give you a notion of trend quality and trend exhaustion
  - seed_pass, fit_error, retracement, overlap, wick burden, and quality bucket are good state features
    for an exit model

  My main suggestions:

  1. Use the predictor to estimate P(trend_continues) and P(trend_ends soon), not just the next class.
     For a grid bot, that is the right target. You care more about:

  - “keep pyramiding”
  - “stop adding”
  - “close all”
    than about exact next-wave label.

  2. Add explicit transition features, not only current-wave features.
     The most useful signal is often the gradient:

  - current trend quality vs previous trend quality
  - seed pass 1 -> 2
  - strong trend followed by weaker trend
  - retracement rising across the last 2-3 trend waves
  - overlap and wick burden increasing while amplitude falls

  That is exactly the kind of sequence HMMs are good at modeling. Hierarchical HMMs are also used for m
  ulti-scale bullish/bearish regime structure, because plain HMMs can confuse short noise with larger r
  egime shifts Oelschläger & Adam, 2020 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14874). HMMs have also been used to
  detect hidden directional trading sequences from transaction streams Vaglica et al., 2010
  (https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.2981).

  3. Add a “do nothing / hold” state to the exit logic.
     For your bot, the decision space should probably be:

  - hold and keep grid active
  - hold but stop adding
  - close basket

  That is better than forcing every state into hold/close. Many bad closes will come from acting too
  early, not too late.

  4. Make the exit model asymmetric.
     Your downside is not symmetric:

  - missing a small early close is often tolerable
  - closing a strong trend too early is expensive
  - closing too late in a reversal can also be expensive

  So train/tune the decision threshold around realized basket PnL, not just label accuracy.

  5. Add basket-aware features.
     Wave-only features are not enough for a grid system. The exit model should also see:

  - current basket size
  - average entry distance from price
  - floating drawdown
  - distance to worst entry
  - time since last add
  - number of stacked positions
  - realized/unrealized convexity of the basket

  Because the same wave state can imply different exit decisions depending on inventory.

  6. Add a higher-timeframe regime input.
     Your 5M waves are useful, but exits improve if the model also knows:

  - 30M or 1H trend state
  - whether 5M trend is aligned with higher timeframe
  - whether current 5M weakening is just local pullback inside a stronger higher-timeframe trend

  That is close to the multi-scale idea behind hierarchical regime models Oelschläger & Adam, 2020
  (https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14874).

  7. Track “trend exhaustion” explicitly.
     I would add a derived feature like:

  - exhaustion_score = rising wicks + rising overlap + falling amplitude + falling DER + increasing
    retracement
    over the last few trend waves

  That may be more actionable for exits than raw class labels.

  8. Backtest causally only.
     Since your segmentation is prefix-causal now, the backtest must also run prefix by prefix. Do not
     train or evaluate exits on full-dataset finalized segmentation, or you’ll overstate performance.

  Model choice:

  - If you want interpretability and state persistence: HMM / hierarchical HMM is a good first choice.
  - If you want raw predictive power later: gradient-boosted trees on wave-sequence features may
    outperform a plain HMM.
  - A strong practical setup is:
      - HMM for latent regime state
      - simple supervised exit policy on top of HMM state + wave features + basket features

  If I had to prioritize one improvement now, it would be:

  - build a causal feature set from the last 3-5 waves
  - include basket state
  - predict close_all vs hold/stop_add
  - not just next wave class

  Sources:

  - Oelschläger & Adam, “Detecting bearish and bullish markets in financial time series using hierarchi
    cal hidden Markov models” (2020): https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14874
  - Vaglica et al., “Statistical identification with hidden Markov models of large order splitting stra
    tegies in an equity market” (2010): https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.2981
